The Four Types of Analytics

🔮
Predictive
"What will happen?" Uses past data and statistical or machine-learning models to forecast outcomes — demand, churn, credit risk.

🧭
Prescriptive
"What should we do?" Recommends specific actions using optimization and simulation — best price, best route, next-best offer.

Exam Trap Order of value: Descriptive → Diagnostic → Predictive → Prescriptive. Prescriptive is the most advanced; descriptive only looks backward.

Chapter 12 · Risks & Ethics
Analytics & AI Risks
Powerful tools need guardrails. Click each risk.
⚖️
Bias
Models learn from historical data — including its unfairness. Biased hiring or lending data produces biased predictions.
Click to reveal
🕳️
Black-Box Decisions
Some models can't explain why they decided something. Regulators and customers demand explainability.
Click to reveal
🔐
Privacy
Combining datasets can reveal sensitive facts about individuals. Collect only what you need and protect it.
Click to reveal
🧪

Poor data gives poor insight; generative AI can invent plausible but false answers. Always verify important outputs.

Chapter 12 · Takeaways
What You Must Know
  • BI collects, integrates and visualizes data (what is happening). Business analytics goes deeper (why and what next). Big data = Volume, Velocity, Variety.
  • Four types: Descriptive (what happened), Diagnostic (why), Predictive (what will happen), Prescriptive (what should we do) — rising in sophistication and value.
  • Data flows through ETL into a data warehouse (data marts for departments); data mining finds hidden patterns.
  • Data visualization and dashboards turn KPIs into pictures so managers can act quickly.
  • AI includes machine learning, NLP and generative AI; ML learns patterns from data instead of fixed rules.
  • Risks: bad data, bias, black-box decisions, privacy and hallucinations. Garbage in, garbage out.
