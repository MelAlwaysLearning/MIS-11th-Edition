Chapter 10 · TPS
Transaction Processing Systems (TPS)
The foundation. Click each idea.
🧾
What a TPS Does
Records routine, high-volume, day-to-day transactions — sales, orders, payroll, bank deposits — as they happen.
Click to reveal
⏱️
Requirements
Must be fast, accurate, reliable and always available (24/7). Downtime means lost sales. Data integrity is critical.
Click to reveal
🏧
Examples
Point-of-sale checkout, ATMs, online order entry, airline reservations, payroll processing, student registration.
Click to reveal
⬆️
Why It Matters
A TPS is the main source of data for every other system. Garbage in the TPS means garbage in every report above it.

ERP is powerful — and one of the riskiest IT projects a company can run.
✅
Benefits
Real-time visibility, one version of the truth, lower duplicate work, faster closing of books, better planning and standardized processes.

💸

Chapter 10 · Takeaways
What You Must Know
  • Functional area IS (accounting, marketing, operations, HR) serve single departments. When they can't share data, they create information silos.
  • TPS records routine, high-volume transactions and is the main source of data for all other systems. It must be fast, accurate and always available.
  • MIS = periodic reports for structured decisions. DSS = models and what-if for semi-structured decisions. EIS/ESS = dashboards with drill-down for senior executives.
  • ERP integrates all core business processes on one shared database, eliminating silos and duplicate entry. Vendors: SAP, Oracle, Microsoft, Workday.
  • ERP projects are costly and risky — failures usually come from poor change management, weak executive support and over-customization.
  • Interorganizational systems, extranets and cloud enterprise systems extend integration beyond one company.
