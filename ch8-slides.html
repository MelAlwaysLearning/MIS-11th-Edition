Chapter 8 · Definitions
Wireless vs. Mobile
Two words that sound identical but mean different things. Click each to see the distinction.
📡
Wireless
Transmitting data without wires — using radio waves, microwaves, or infrared. A desktop PC on Wi-Fi is wireless but not mobile.
Click to reveal
🚶
Mobile
The device or user moves while staying connected. A laptop carried from class to the library is mobile. Mobile devices are often — but not always — wireless.
Click to reveal
📱
Mobile Computing
Real-time, wireless connection between a mobile device and other computing environments — the internet, an intranet, or another device. Smartphones, tablets, wearables.
Click to reveal
🔑
Key Distinction
Wireless = no cables. Mobile = it moves.

Smart-home thermostat: wireless, not mobile. Phone on Wi-Fi in your hand: both. A laptop plugged into an Ethernet jack in a hotel: mobile, not wireless.

Exam Trap Wireless ≠ mobile. Wireless describes the connection; mobile describes the movement. Questions love to give you a stationary wireless device.

Chapter 8 · Short-Range Wireless
Short-Range Wireless Technologies
Measured in centimeters to meters. Click each technology to see how it works.
🎧
Bluetooth
Short-range (~10 m typical) wireless that links nearby devices in a personal area network (PAN). Headphones, keyboards, smartwatches, car hands-free.
Click to reveal
💳
NFC
Near Field Communication — a few centimeters. Two devices must nearly touch. Powers Apple Pay / Google Pay tap-to-pay, transit passes, and digital keys.
Click to reveal
🏷️
RFID
Radio Frequency Identification — a tag holds data, a reader powers and reads it with radio waves. No line of sight needed. Inventory, toll passes (E-ZPass / SunPass), pet chips.
Click to reveal
📶
Ultra-Wideband (UWB)
Very precise short-range location using pulses of radio energy. Finds items to within centimeters — AirTag precision finding and some digital car keys use it.
Click to reveal
Also Know Passive RFID tags have no battery — the reader's signal powers them. Active tags have a battery and a much longer range.

Chapter 8 · Team Competition
Which Wireless Technology?
2 points per correct answer. Pick the best technology for each scenario.
Scenario 1
You tap your phone on a coffee shop reader and the payment goes through instantly. Which technology, and why must the phone be so close?
NFC. Its range is only a few centimeters — which is a security feature. The two devices have to nearly touch, making accidental or remote interception much harder.
Click to reveal
Scenario 2
A warehouse needs to count 10,000 boxes on a pallet without opening a single one. Which technology fits?
RFID. Readers pick up many tags at once with radio waves and need no line of sight, unlike barcodes that must be scanned one at a time.
Click to reveal
Scenario 3
Your wireless earbuds connect to your phone. What type of network have you created, and which technology made it?
A personal area network (PAN) using Bluetooth. Very short range, low power, connects personal devices around one person.
Click to reveal
Scenario 4
A coffee shop gives customers internet access across the whole store. Which technology, and what network type?
Wi-Fi creating a wireless local area network (WLAN). Medium range (tens of meters), connects many devices to the internet through an access point.

Chapter 8 · Medium & Wide-Range Wireless
Medium & Wide-Range Wireless
From a coffee shop to a whole country. Click each to see what it does.
🏠
Wi-Fi
Wireless local area networking (WLAN) using access points. Range of tens of meters indoors. Fast and cheap but limited to a building or campus. Hotspots in cafés, airports, FIU.
Click to reveal
🕸️
Mesh Network
Multiple nodes relay signals to each other, covering a large area without long cable runs. Home mesh routers eliminate dead spots; cities use mesh for public Wi-Fi.
Click to reveal
🗼
Cellular
Wide-area radio network of towers (cells). Carries voice and data over long distances while you move between cells. 3G → 4G LTE → 5G.
Click to reveal
🛰️
Satellite & GPS
Satellites cover remote areas where towers can't reach. GPS uses satellites to tell your device exactly where it is — the foundation of navigation and location-based services.
Click to reveal
Exam Note Match range to network type: PAN (Bluetooth) → LAN (Wi-Fi) → WAN (cellular, satellite). Bigger coverage generally means slower and costlier per device.

Chapter 8 · Security
Wireless & Mobile Security Threats
Radio waves travel through the air, so anyone nearby can try to listen. Click each threat.
🎭
Rogue Access Point
An unauthorized access point connected to a network, or a fake "Free_Airport_WiFi" that tricks people into joining. The attacker can read or alter their traffic.
Click to reveal
👂
Eavesdropping
Unauthorized interception of data sent over the air. Unencrypted public Wi-Fi is the classic risk. Defense: encryption (WPA3, HTTPS) and a VPN.
Click to reveal
📡
RF Jamming
Deliberately flooding the radio frequencies with noise so legitimate signals can't get through — a wireless denial-of-service attack.
Click to reveal
📱
Lost & Stolen Devices
Phones carry email, banking and photos. Defense: screen lock, biometrics, full-device encryption, remote wipe, and keeping apps updated.
Click to reveal
Exam Trap Free public Wi-Fi without a VPN is not safe for banking. HTTPS protects the page you visit, but a rogue hotspot can still see which sites you use.

Chapter 8 · Takeaways
What You Must Know
  • Wireless = transmitting without wires. Mobile = the device/user moves. They are not the same — a desktop on Wi-Fi is wireless but not mobile.
  • Short range: NFC (a few cm, tap-to-pay), Bluetooth (~10 m, PAN), RFID (tags + readers, no line of sight). Medium: Wi-Fi (WLAN). Wide: cellular (3G/4G/5G) and satellite/GPS.
  • RFID tags can be passive (powered by the reader) or active (battery-powered). RFID reads many items at once without line of sight — barcodes cannot.
  • M-commerce = e-commerce on mobile devices: mobile banking and payments, location-based services, mobile ads, tickets. Location awareness is its key advantage.
  • Pervasive computing and the Internet of Things embed sensors and networking in everyday objects; wireless sensor networks collect the data.
  • Threats: rogue access points, eavesdropping, RF jamming, lost devices. Defenses: WPA3/HTTPS encryption, VPN, screen lock, remote wipe.
