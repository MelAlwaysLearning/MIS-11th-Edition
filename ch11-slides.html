Chapter 11 · Problems & Solutions
Supply Chain Problems & Fixes
Why supply chains break — and what technology does about it.
ProblemWhat HappensHow IS Helps
Bullwhip effect Small changes in consumer demand turn into huge swings in orders as you move upstream Share real-time sales data with all partners; better forecasting
Uncertainty Late shipments, quality issues, demand swings Safety stock, tracking visibility, multiple suppliers
Excess inventory Cash tied up in unsold goods and storage Just-in-Time (JIT) and vendor-managed inventory
Stockouts Customers can't buy what they want Demand forecasting, automatic reordering (EDI)
Lack of visibility Nobody sees where goods are RFID, GPS tracking, supply chain dashboards
Key Point The bullwhip effect is the big textbook concept: distorted demand information gets amplified at every step upstream. Sharing information is the cure.

Chapter 11 · Inventory Strategy
Inventory: Just-in-Time vs. Just-in-Case
How much stock should you hold? Click each strategy.
⏰
Just-in-Time (JIT)
Receive materials only when needed in production. Minimizes storage cost and waste (Toyota). Risky if a supplier is late.
Click to reveal
🛡️
Safety Stock
Extra inventory held to protect against uncertainty. Costs money to store, but prevents stockouts when demand or supply surprises you.
Click to reveal
🤲
Vendor-Managed Inventory
The supplier monitors the customer's stock and decides when to replenish, using shared data. Walmart and P&G are the classic example.
Click to reveal
🏷️
RFID & Tracking
Tags on pallets and items give real-time counts and locations, cutting errors and theft and speeding audits.

Chapter 11 · Takeaways
What You Must Know
  • CRM manages all customer interactions to build loyalty and lifetime value. SCM manages the flow of goods, information and finances from suppliers to customers.
  • CRM types: operational (sales, marketing, service automation), analytical (data mining, churn, CLV), collaborative (shared across channels and partners).
  • CLV = total revenue/profit a customer generates over the full relationship. One view of the customer lets every employee serve them consistently.
  • A supply chain has material, information and financial flows, with upstream suppliers and downstream distribution.
  • The bullwhip effect: small demand changes amplify upstream. Fix with shared information and better forecasting.
  • JIT reduces inventory cost but raises supply risk; safety stock and vendor-managed inventory balance it. RFID, EDI and ERP increase visibility.
