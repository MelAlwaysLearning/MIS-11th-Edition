Chapter 9 · Crowdsourcing
The Wisdom (and Work) of Crowds
Companies tap large online communities for ideas, money and labor. Click each form.
💡
Crowdsourcing
Getting work, ideas, or input from a large online community instead of employees or a single contractor. Idea contests, product feedback, translation, mapping.
Click to reveal
💰
Crowdfunding
Raising small amounts of money from many people online. Kickstarter, GoFundMe, Indiegogo. Backers fund a project in exchange for rewards, equity or just support.
Click to reveal
🧠
Collective Intelligence
The idea that a diverse group can solve problems better than most individuals — as with prediction markets, Wikipedia and open-source software.
Click to reveal
⭐
Ratings & Reviews
Crowd opinion drives purchases: Yelp, Amazon reviews, TripAdvisor. Powerful — and open to fake reviews, which businesses must police.
Click to reveal
Exam Trap Crowdsourcing ≠ outsourcing. Outsourcing hands a task to a specific company; crowdsourcing opens it to an undefined, large online group.

Chapter 9 · Takeaways
What You Must Know
  • Web 2.0 = the interactive web defined by user-generated content. Social computing uses IT to support social interaction, communities and collaboration.
  • Core tools: social networks, blogs/microblogs, wikis (anyone can edit), media sharing. A mashup combines content from several sources into a new service.
  • Crowdsourcing = getting input or work from a large online community (not outsourcing). Crowdfunding raises small amounts of money from many people.
  • Businesses use social computing for marketing, customer service, product feedback, recruiting and internal collaboration. Social commerce lets people shop inside platforms.
  • Risks: privacy loss, misinformation and deepfakes, trolls and cyberbullying, brand reputation damage, and fake reviews.
  • Safeguards: social media policy, social listening, content moderation, and employee training. Disclose sponsored content.
